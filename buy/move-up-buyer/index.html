Move-up buyer

You need to sell to buy, and buy to sell. The order of operations is the entire deal, and it is where most move-ups get stuck.

What is actually true here

  • The four exits exist precisely for this — a certain off-market or portfolio sale can unlock a purchase that a contingent listing cannot
  • A contingent offer is weaker in a market where the median Lane County house went pending in 18 days in June 2026 (Redfin)
  • Bridge financing, a rent-back, and a delayed close are all normal tools here

How we work this

We model sell-first and buy-first with your actual numbers, then use the four exits to manufacture the certainty a clean offer requires. A sale with a date you control is worth more in a competing-offer situation than a slightly higher price.

Questions

Asked and answered

Should I sell first or buy first?

There is no universal answer, and anyone who gives you one is guessing. Selling first removes all timing risk and costs you a move or a rent-back. Buying first is cleaner if you can carry both. We model both with your actual numbers.

Can you make my offer non-contingent?

Sometimes. An off-market or portfolio sale of your current house can close on a date you control, which turns a contingent offer into a clean one. That is one of the specific reasons the four exits exist.

What does a rent-back actually cost me?

A rent-back is usually priced at the buyer’s carrying cost per day, and it needs to be negotiated into the offer rather than asked for later. It is one of the cheapest ways to buy yourself three weeks.
