The thresholds

Three numbers decide
which path you’re on.

Oregon's small estate affidavit is available when the estate's fair market value is $275,000 or less, with no more than $200,000 in real property and no more than $75,000 in personal property. All three limits must hold at once, and nothing can be filed until 30 days after the date of death.

Lane County's median sale price is around $464,000. For most estates with a house in them, that rules the short path out.

Selling the house

You can usually sell
before the case closes.

Under ORS 114.325, unless the will provides otherwise, the personal representative may sell or convey real property without a court hearing, with stated exceptions. Sale may be necessary to pay spousal support, to pay child support, to fund the elective share of a surviving spouse, to pay claims and expenses of administration.

Waiting until the estate closes means paying insurance, utilities, taxes and yard care on an empty house for months, for no benefit. That carrying cost is real money out of the distribution.

Where it’s filed

Oregon probate is filed in the circuit court for a county, and which county depends on where the decedent lived and where the property sits. Your attorney confirms the venue before anything is filed.

Lane County Circuit Court

125 East 8th Avenue, Eugene, OR 97401

Probate Department, 2nd floor

Every other Oregon county files in its own circuit court — the county seats are listed by region.
