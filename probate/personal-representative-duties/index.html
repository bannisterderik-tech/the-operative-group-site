What a personal representative has to do

You’re a fiduciary now. That’s a legal standard, and it applies to how you sell the house.

A personal representative inventories the estate, notifies heirs and creditors, handles claims, manages assets during administration, and ultimately distributes what’s left. Selling real property is one task inside that.

The fiduciary standard is why a documented, defensible sale process matters. If one heir later questions the price, the answer should be a file — a CMA, a marketing record, the offers received — not a recollection.

Not legal advice. This is general information about Oregon probate, not legal advice. Every estate is different. Talk to an Oregon probate attorney about your specific situation — we’ll refer you to one.
Where it’s filedOregon probate is filed in the circuit court for a county, and which county depends on where the decedent lived and where the property sits. Your attorney confirms the venue before anything is filed. In Lane County that’s the Lane County Circuit Court, 125 East 8th Avenue, Eugene, OR 97401 (Probate Department, 2nd floor). Every other Oregon county files in its own circuit court — county seats by region.

Questions

Asked and answered

Can I sell to myself or to a relative?

That’s exactly the transaction a fiduciary standard is designed to scrutinize. Get attorney guidance, get an independent valuation, and document everything.

Do all the heirs have to agree on the price?

Not necessarily, but a personal representative who ignores the heirs usually ends up in a dispute. Transparency is cheaper than litigation.

Am I personally liable?

You can be, for breaches of duty. This is why the process and the paper trail matter.
