Oregon Probate Path Finder

Five questions, built on the actual Oregon thresholds and the Lane County Circuit Court schedule. Nothing is stored and nothing is sent anywhere.

Step 1 of 5

1. Was the property held in a trust, or does it pass by beneficiary designation or right of survivorship?

2. Has it been at least 30 days since the date of death?

Oregon’s small estate affidavit can’t be filed until 30 days after the date of death.

3. What is the fair market value of the real property in the estate?

Lane County’s median sale price is around $464,000, so a house alone usually exceeds this.

4. What is the value of the personal property — accounts, vehicles, contents?

Questions

Asked and answered

Is this legal advice?

No. It’s a plain-English map of the published Oregon thresholds and filing fees. Every estate has details this can’t see. Talk to an Oregon probate attorney — we’ll refer you to one who works in Lane County.

Where do the numbers come from?

Oregon’s small estate affidavit limits ($275,000 total, $200,000 real property, $75,000 personal property, 30-day wait), the Oregon Judicial Department filing fees ($124 affidavit, $298 typical formal petition for estates from $50,000 to $1 million), and ORS 114.325 on a personal representative’s authority to sell real property.

Does the house count toward the limits?

Yes, at fair market value. Lane County’s median sale price is around $464,000, which is why most estates with a house exceed the $200,000 real property limit and go to formal probate.

Can I sell the house before probate closes?

Usually, yes. Under ORS 114.325, unless the will provides otherwise, the personal representative may sell or convey real property without a court hearing, with stated exceptions. Your attorney confirms it for your case.
