APOD & cap rate calculator

Gross scheduled income down to net operating income, then the three metrics: cap rate, gross rent multiplier, cash-on-cash. Defaults are conservative on purpose — a pro forma that omits vacancy, management and reserves is a marketing document, not an underwriting.

Inputs
Scheduled rent under the current leases, not market rent
Laundry, storage, parking, pet rent
Water, sewer, trash, common power, landscaping
Financing
Annual property operating data

Not investment advice

An estimate from the figures you enter. It’s not an appraisal, not a valuation, and not investment advice. Verify rents against the actual leases, expenses against actual statements, taxes against the county record, and insurance against a real quote before you rely on any of it.

Questions

Asked and answered

Why are the default expense assumptions so high?

Because the ones on listing flyers are usually too low. Vacancy, management and capital reserves are real costs whether or not you pay them to somebody else. A deal that only works with them removed doesn’t work.

Should I include management if I self-manage?

Yes. Your time has a price, and if you ever stop self-managing the expense appears. Leaving it out makes a bad deal look like a good one.

What cap rate is good in Lane County?

There’s no single right answer — it depends on the asset class, the vintage, and what you’re being paid to take on. The useful comparison is against other deals you can buy right now, and against what your money does elsewhere.

Does this account for the Oregon rent cap?

Not directly — it values the property as it operates today. When you model rent growth, remember Oregon caps most long-term residential increases at 9.5% for 2026 under ORS 90.323, and 6% for manufactured home facilities with more than 30 spaces.
