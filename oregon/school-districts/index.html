Reference

Oregon school districts

All 173 of them, sorted by county, with the administrative facts and nothing else. No ratings, no scores, no demographics.

Short answer

Oregon has 173 public school districts serving about 542,625 students. The largest is Portland SD 1J with about 44,740; the smallest is Suntex SD 10 with 2. District boundaries do not follow city limits or postal codes — only the district office can confirm which district an address is in.

Urban Institute Education Data Portal / NCES Common Core of Data, 2022, retrieved September 2026

Checked September 2026

Why there are no ratings here

Every real estate site in the country puts a number on the schools next to the price. We don’t, and the reason isn’t squeamishness.

Section 3604(c) of the Fair Housing Act reaches the advertising of housing, not only the sale of it. HUD and the National Association of Realtors both treat school-quality language as a proxy for race and familial status, because in practice that’s what it does: it tells some buyers where they belong and others where they don’t, without anyone having to say so. A brokerage that calls the schools good beside a listing has made a steering statement whether it meant to or not.

So these pages carry the administrative facts — which district, where the office is, how many schools, how many students, which grades. The federal dataset behind them also breaks out special-education, English-learner and migrant enrolment, and staffing ratios. None of that is published here either, for the same reason: those numbers proxy disability and national origin, and they have no business sitting next to a house for sale.

Go and see the schools. Talk to the district. That judgment is yours to make and it isn’t ours to make for you.

Questions

Asked and answered

How many school districts are in Oregon?

173 regular public school districts, serving about 542,625 students between them. Several counties have a dozen or more.

Does a ZIP code tell me the school district?

No. Postal codes are USPS delivery routes and district boundaries were drawn separately over more than a century. They cross each other constantly. Only the district office can confirm an address.

Why do you not rank the schools?

Because ranking schools next to property listings steers buyers, and steering on familial status or race breaches the Fair Housing Act whether or not it was intended. We give you the district and you make your own judgment.
