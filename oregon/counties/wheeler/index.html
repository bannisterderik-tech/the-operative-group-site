Wheeler County

Fossil is the county seat. Wheeler County was established in 1899 and its circuit court hears every probate and title action for property inside its lines.

Short answer

Wheeler County covers 1,715 square miles with about 1,472 residents, 3 incorporated cities and its county seat at Fossil. Real estate here is governed by Oregon statute, so the rent cap, middle-housing rules and probate procedure are the same as in Eugene — and we place you here through the referral network.

The shape of the county

At 1 people per square mile this is frontier-density country. Comparable sales can be miles apart and months old, which changes how a price gets built: an appraiser here works from a much wider radius than one in Eugene, and so should you.

The county holds 3 incorporated cities, from Fossil at 447 down to Mitchell at 138. That spread matters: which side of a city limit a parcel falls on decides its zoning, its utilities and often its taxes.

What Oregon law does here

No city in Wheeler County reaches the 25,000-person threshold that triggers the full HB 2001 middle-housing requirements, so local zoning governs what can be built on a residential lot here.

The statewide 9.5% rent cap under ORS 90.323 reaches Wheeler County exactly as it reaches Lane. Wheeler County sits in Oregon’s 7th judicial district, which it shares with Gilliam County, Hood River County, Sherman County and Wasco County. Cases are still filed in the Wheeler County Circuit Court at Fossil — sharing a district means sharing judges and administration, not a courthouse. Probate in Wheeler County.

How much of the county is actually in a city

About 49% of Wheeler County’s residents live inside a city limit, which leaves roughly 748 people on unincorporated county land. That split is the single most practical thing to establish about a parcel here. Unincorporated property answers to the county planning department, usually sits on a well and a septic system, and is assessed by the county with no city levy on top.

The county’s oldest incorporated city is Fossil, from 1891; the newest is Spray, from 1958. That 67-year spread is visible in the housing stock, and it is the first thing an inspection report tells you.

3 postal codes cover the county, spread across roughly 25 miles north to south and 16 miles east to west. Every Oregon postal code is indexed by county.

Questions

Asked and answered

Where is Wheeler County’s county seat?

Fossil. The county seat holds the county offices and the Wheeler County Circuit Court, which is where probate and title actions for property in this county are filed.

How many people live in Wheeler County?

About 1,472, across 1,715 square miles — roughly 1 people per square mile. That makes it the 36th most populous of Oregon’s 36 counties. Figures retrieved September 2026.

Does the Oregon rent cap apply in Wheeler County?

Yes. ORS 90.323 is a statewide statute, so the 9.5% cap, the 15-year new-construction exemption and the 90-day notice requirement apply here the same way they apply in Eugene.

Do you work in Wheeler County?

Not with our own boots on the ground. We place you with an agent who works Wheeler County through REAL Broker’s referral network, and we stay involved until the handover’s clean.
