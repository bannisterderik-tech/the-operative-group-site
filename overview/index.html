Fair housing, and one decision you should know about

Every page on this site is checked against 36 fair-housing rules before it can be published. They cover the seven federal protected classes and the extra ones Oregon adds under ORS 659A.421, plus the proxy language enforcement actions actually turn on — the phrases about who a place suits, how safe it is, which areas are on the way up, and which are exclusive. The check reads the visible words, the image alt text, the meta tags and the structured data. If anything trips it, the site doesn't build. Every page also carries the Equal Housing Opportunity statement, because the law reaches advertising and not only the sale.

The school pages carry no ratings, and that was deliberate. The federal dataset behind them also contains special-education, English-learner and migrant enrolment, and teacher staffing ratios per district. None of it was imported. Those numbers stand in for disability and national origin, and staffing ratios are what the phrase every listing site uses about schools is built from — which HUD and the National Association of Realtors both treat as a proxy for race and family status. Publishing them next to houses for sale is how brokerages end up defending a complaint.

Every competitor in this market puts a number on the schools. Not showing one is a competitive decision as well as a legal one: it is the difference between a site that can be handed to a compliance officer and one that cannot. If you want the schools ranked on this site, that is a conversation to have before anything ships, not after.

Worth knowingThe gate has blocked the build five times on copy written for this site — including on this page, because explaining why the site ranks no schools meant writing the banned phrases. Every one was reworded rather than waved through. A gate with exceptions in it stops being a gate.
